Medicare Advantage OTC and Flex Card Allowances in California: What's Really Included

By Susana Marcos, Licensed Medicare Agent · Published August 22, 2026 · 7 min read

The OTC or flex card allowance is one of the most talked-about perks in Medicare Advantage marketing, and also one of the most misunderstood. Commercials show a card loaded with hundreds of dollars, but they rarely explain how the money can actually be spent, whether it expires, or how much it really varies from plan to plan. If you're comparing plans before AEP, understanding what's behind that allowance matters more than the headline dollar figure.

Here's what these cards actually cover, how they differ between carriers, and what to check before you assume a bigger number means a better deal.

What an OTC Allowance Actually Covers

An OTC, or over-the-counter, allowance is a set amount loaded onto a card on a monthly or quarterly basis, meant to be spent on approved health items without a copay. Typical approved categories include pain relievers, vitamins and supplements, first aid supplies, cold and allergy medication, and at-home test kits. The list of eligible items is set by your specific plan, not by Medicare broadly, so two plans from different carriers can have noticeably different approved item lists even with a similar dollar allowance.

How a Flex Card Differs From a Straight OTC Card

A flex card is typically broader than an OTC-only card. Depending on the plan, a flex card allowance can be used for healthy groceries, utility bills, or dental, vision, and hearing costs in addition to over-the-counter items. Some California plans issue a single combined card with separate spending buckets for each category, while others issue OTC and flex benefits as entirely separate cards with their own balances and rules.

OTC card

Generally limited to health and wellness products found at a pharmacy: pain relief, vitamins, first aid, cold and flu items.

Flex card

Broader spending categories that can include groceries, utilities, or out-of-pocket dental, vision, and hearing costs, depending on the plan.

Does the Balance Roll Over or Disappear?

This is the detail that trips people up most often. Some plans load the allowance monthly and any unused balance is lost at the end of the month. Others accumulate the allowance on a quarterly or annual basis, giving you more flexibility to save up for a bigger purchase. This detail is spelled out in your plan's Evidence of Coverage, but it's rarely emphasized in marketing materials, so it's worth asking directly when comparing plans.

Where You Can Actually Use the Card

Most OTC and flex cards work at major pharmacy chains and select grocery stores that carry approved items, and many plans also allow online ordering through a dedicated portal or a mail-order catalog. Coverage of specific retailers can vary by county, so it's worth checking your plan's approved retailer list before you count on using the card at a particular store near you. Before comparing OTC or flex allowances between plans:

  • Confirm whether the balance is monthly, quarterly, or annual, and whether unused funds roll over
  • Ask for the plan's specific list of approved items and categories
  • Check which stores in your area accept the card
  • Compare the dollar amount alongside the plan's premium, deductible, and provider network, not on its own

Don't Let the Allowance Distract From the Bigger Picture

A large OTC or flex allowance can make a plan look more generous at a glance, but it shouldn't be the deciding factor on its own. A plan with a smaller allowance but a wider provider network, lower drug costs, or a lower maximum out-of-pocket could easily save you more overall. Frequently Asked Questions

What is an OTC allowance on a Medicare Advantage plan?

A set dollar amount loaded onto a card monthly or quarterly for approved health items like pain relievers, vitamins, bandages, and home test kits, without a copay.

What's the difference between an OTC card and a flex card?

An OTC card usually covers only over-the-counter health products. A flex card is broader and can include groceries, utilities, or dental, vision, and hearing costs, depending on the plan.

Does unused OTC or flex card balance roll over to the next month?

It depends on the plan. Some reset monthly, others accumulate quarterly or annually. Check your Evidence of Coverage or ask your agent.

Where can I use my Medicare Advantage OTC or flex card in California?

Most cards work at major pharmacy chains and some grocery stores, with online ordering often available. Your plan's portal lists approved retailers near you.

Can I get cash back from an OTC or flex card balance?

No. These allowances can only be spent on approved items or services through approved retailers, not withdrawn as cash.

One More Thought

The OTC or flex card is a genuinely useful perk, but it's a supporting benefit, not the main event. Before AEP, it's worth understanding exactly how your allowance works and comparing it against the plan features that affect your health costs the most.

Share this with someone you love. If a parent or family member has an OTC or flex card sitting unused, a quick conversation now could help them get real value out of it before the balance resets.
