Frequently Asked

Medicare Questions Answered

You become eligible for Medicare at age 65. Your Initial Enrollment Period (IEP) begins three months before your 65th birthday month and ends three months after. Enrolling on time is important because delaying without a qualifying exception can result in permanently higher premiums. You can also make changes during the Annual Enrollment Period (AEP), October 15 through December 7 each year, or during a Special Enrollment Period (SEP) if you qualify due to a life event such as moving or losing employer coverage.
Medicare Advantage (Part C) is an all-in-one alternative to Original Medicare offered by private insurers. It bundles Part A, Part B, and usually Part D prescription drug coverage into a single plan, often with added benefits like dental, vision, and hearing. These plans tend to have lower monthly premiums but may have network restrictions (HMO or PPO) and may require prior authorization for specific procedures. Medigap (Medicare Supplement) works alongside Original Medicare to cover out-of-pocket costs like copayments, coinsurance, and deductibles.
