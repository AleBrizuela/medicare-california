How to Choose a Trustworthy Medicare Agent in California Before AEP

When October arrives, many people start getting calls, letters, and even home visits from insurance agents offering to help with their Medicare. Some of those agents genuinely work with your best interest in mind. Others are just trying to hit a sales quota with whichever carrier pays the highest commission. The difference between the two can cost you hundreds of dollars a year, or leave you without the doctor you need.

This article walks through the warning signs to watch for, and the exact questions to ask before any agent enrolls you in a Medicare plan this fall.

Why It Matters Who Helps You Choose Your Plan

Choosing a Medicare Advantage or Medigap plan is not like buying car insurance. The decisions you make affect which doctors you can see, how much you pay for your medications, and what extra benefits you get for the entire following year. A good agent helps you compare options based on your actual situation. A bad agent pushes you toward whichever plan benefits them, not you.

To understand the difference between the types of plans an agent might offer, take a look at our Medicare Advantage in California guide.

Agents Don't Cost You Anything, but Not All of Them Work the Same Way

First, something many people don't realize: using a Medicare agent is free. Agents are paid by the insurance companies, not by you, and your premium is exactly the same whether you enroll through an agent or directly with the carrier. This means there is no reason to distrust using an agent, but there is good reason to be selective about which one.

Single-carrier agent

Can only offer you plans from that one insurance company, even if they are not the best fit based on your medications and doctors.

Independent or multi-carrier agency agent

Is contracted with several insurance companies and can compare plans across carriers to find the one that actually fits your specific situation.

Four Warning Signs to Watch For

1. They Offer You Only One Plan Without Comparing Options

If an agent presents you with a single plan and calls it "the best one" without asking about your medications, doctors, or budget, that is the first warning sign. An agent who is genuinely working for you compares at least two or three options before making a recommendation.

2. They Don't Ask About Your Medications or Doctors

Every plan has its own drug formulary and its own provider network. Without knowing what medications you take and which doctors you see, no agent can truly know which plan fits you. If an agent enrolls you without asking these basic questions, they are not doing their job properly.

3. They Pressure You to Decide Immediately

A good agent gives you time to think, even if that means you decide to call them back after comparing with someone else. If you feel pressured to sign on the first call or visit, with no time to review the information calmly or talk it over with family, treat that as a serious warning sign.

4. They Can't Explain Their License or Certifications

Every insurance agent in California must hold an active license from the California Department of Insurance (CDI), and must also be certified each year by every carrier whose plans they sell. A trustworthy agent shares their license number without hesitation and has no problem with you verifying it.

Questions to ask an agent before enrolling:

  • How many insurance companies are you contracted with?
  • Can you review my specific medications and doctors?
  • What is your California Department of Insurance license number?
  • Why are you recommending this plan over the others?
  • Can I call you after AEP if I have questions or issues?

Verify the license: you can confirm any agent's license number on the California Department of Insurance (CDI) website. It takes less than a minute and gives you peace of mind before sharing your personal information.

Medicare Advantage or Medigap: A Good Agent Explains Both

Another sign of a good agent is that they explain your options beyond Medicare Advantage. If a Medigap (Medicare Supplement) plan fits your lifestyle better, an honest agent will mention it, even though that sale pays a different commission. If an agent only talks about one type of plan without mentioning the other, ask directly why.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to use a Medicare agent?

Nothing. Agents are paid by the insurance companies, not the client, and you pay the same premium with or without an agent.

How do I know if a Medicare agent is licensed?

Ask for their California Department of Insurance license number and verify it on the CDI website.

Is an independent agent better than one working for a single company?

An independent or multi-carrier agency agent can compare plans across several insurers, while a single-carrier agent can only offer their own plans.

What should I ask an agent before enrolling?

How many carriers they're contracted with, whether they'll review your medications and doctors, their license number, and why they recommend that plan over others.

Can I switch agents if I'm already enrolled with one?

Yes. You can work with whichever agent you prefer at any time, and switching agents doesn't change your plan or coverage.

One More Thought

A good Medicare agent isn't the one who sells you a plan the fastest. It's the one who asks the right questions, compares real options, and stays available after you sign. Spending a few minutes verifying who is helping you can save you money, time, and frustration for the entire year ahead.
