Does Medicare Cover You When You Travel Outside California?

Summer is travel season. Whether you are visiting grandchildren in another state, taking a long-awaited cruise, or spending part of the year away from California, one question matters before you pack: does your Medicare go with you? The answer depends on which kind of Medicare you have. Original Medicare, Medicare Advantage, and Medigap each treat travel very differently, and knowing the rules can save you from a surprise bill far from home.

Original Medicare Travels With You Across the Country

If you have Original Medicare (Part A and Part B), the good news is simple: it works anywhere in the United States and its territories. You can see any doctor, clinic, or hospital that accepts Medicare, in any of the 50 states, Puerto Rico, Guam, the U.S. Virgin Islands, and more. Your benefits and your costs stay the same whether you are in Los Angeles or Miami.

This nationwide freedom is one of the main reasons some California seniors keep Original Medicare. There is no network to worry about and no referral to chase. As long as the provider accepts Medicare, you are covered the same way you would be at home.

Tip: Before a long trip, confirm your provider at your destination "accepts assignment." Providers who accept assignment agree to Medicare's approved amount, which keeps your out-of-pocket cost predictable.

Medicare Advantage Is Tied to Your Local Network

Medicare Advantage (Part C) plans work differently. These plans are built around local provider networks tied to the California county where you live. That structure keeps premiums low and adds extras like dental and vision, but it can become a problem when you leave the area.

For most Medicare Advantage plans, here is how travel inside the United States works:

  • Emergencies and urgent care: Always covered, anywhere in the country, even outside your network. This is required by law.
  • Routine and planned care: Often not covered out of network, or covered only at a higher cost. A routine check-up while visiting another state may come out of your pocket.
  • Prescriptions: Usually fine at national pharmacy chains, but confirm your plan's pharmacy network before you leave.

If you split your year between California and another state, ask your plan whether it offers a travel benefit or a national network. Some larger plans do. If you are weighing your options, our guide to the best Medicare Advantage plans in California explains how networks affect everyday care.

Medigap: Built for People Who Move Around

If you have Original Medicare plus a Medigap (Medicare Supplement) policy, you get the best of both worlds for travel. Medigap works with any provider nationwide that accepts Medicare, and several Medigap plan types add a benefit Original Medicare does not include: foreign travel emergency coverage.

Plans like Medigap Plan G and Plan N typically cover 80% of approved emergency care during the first 60 days of a trip outside the United States, up to a lifetime limit, after a small deductible. That can matter on a cruise or an overseas visit. To see how supplements compare for California residents, read our Medigap in California guide.

Traveling Outside the United States

This is where many travelers get caught off guard. Original Medicare generally does not cover care received outside the country, with only a few narrow exceptions. So a hospital visit in Mexico, Italy, or the Philippines would usually not be paid by Medicare.

You have a few ways to protect yourself for international trips:

  • Choose a Medigap plan that includes foreign travel emergency coverage.
  • Check whether your Medicare Advantage plan adds a worldwide emergency benefit. Some do.
  • Buy a separate travel medical insurance policy for the trip. These are inexpensive and fill the gap Medicare leaves abroad.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Original Medicare work in other states?

Yes. Original Medicare (Part A and Part B) works anywhere in the United States and its territories. You can see any doctor or hospital that accepts Medicare. Your coverage and costs do not change when you cross state lines.

Will my California Medicare Advantage plan cover me out of state?

It depends on your plan. Most Medicare Advantage plans use local networks tied to your California county, so out-of-state care may be covered only for emergencies and urgent care. Routine visits while traveling are often not covered, or are covered at a higher cost.

Does Medicare cover me if I travel outside the United States?

Generally no. Original Medicare does not cover care received outside the country, except in rare situations. Some Medigap plans include limited foreign travel emergency coverage, and some Medicare Advantage plans add a worldwide emergency benefit.

Is emergency care covered while traveling on Medicare?

Yes, within the United States. Both Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage plans must cover emergency and urgent care anywhere in the country, even outside your plan's network.

One More Thought

Travel should be the fun part of retirement, not a source of medical worry. A few minutes spent confirming how your plan handles trips can save you a major bill and a lot of stress.
